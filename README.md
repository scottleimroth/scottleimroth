## 👋 Hi, I'm Scott

Associate Lecturer and PhD candidate, School of Psychology, University of Wollongong.
B.Mus., B.Ed., B.Psych.Sci., B.Psych. (Hons.)

### 🎵 Research

Psychophysiology and music. My doctoral work is on **cardiovascular measures of cognition
in response to music** — heart rate variability, skin conductance, and what they tell us
about how listeners actually process what they hear.

Always glad to hear from anyone working in the same area.

### 🧠 Local-first AI

**Local first, not local only.** I run open-weight models on my own hardware and reach for
a frontier API when the local model genuinely isn't the right tool — not by default, and
not never.

What I actually work on:

- **NVIDIA DGX Spark (GB10 / sm_121a)** — vLLM serving, speculative decoding, NVFP4 and
  INT4 quantisation, and the specific ways aarch64 Blackwell differs from everything the
  documentation assumes.
- **AMD Strix Point (Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 / Radeon 890M)** — see
  [geekom-benchmarks](https://github.com/scottleimroth/geekom-benchmarks).
- **Benchmarking that survives contact with reality.** A single tok/s number tells you
  almost nothing. I care about pass rates across repeated runs, structured-output
  reliability, tool-calling that doesn't quietly fail, and catching the runaway generation
  that a one-shot test never sees.
- **Agentic workflows** for research and for running the whole thing — most of what I build
  gets built with models rather than by hand.

The DGX Spark is a niche box. NVIDIA sells it and moves on; the model labs ship and move on.
Nearly everything that makes it genuinely usable comes from a small circle of independent
owners publishing recipes and patches for free. I try to put findings back where I found
them.

### 📡 Also interested in

RF and sensing — RTL-SDR sky scanning, Wi-Fi sensing, underwater and diver-portable sonar,
wearable physiological data. Mostly the same question in different clothes: what can you
measure with cheap hardware and patience?
